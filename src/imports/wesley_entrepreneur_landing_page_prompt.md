# 3. The Origin Story — This Is the Most Important Section

This section needs to preserve the emotional and personal origin of the idea.

Do NOT make this sound like startup marketing copy.

It should feel like a genuine founder telling someone how the idea started.

The story:

I started playing a simple game with my dad.

Every Friday before an F1 weekend, we would sit down together and make our bets.

We used a printed-out spreadsheet and went through the Formula 1 grid, deciding what we thought would happen.

We started calling it **"Friday Betting."**

That is where the name **FR1BET** came from.

The important part was not simply predicting the winner.

When we sat down to decide what we wanted to bet on, we started asking a bigger question:

> **When people follow the F1 grid, what are they really watching for?**

Is it the podium?

The strategy?

The split-second battles?

Or the moment everything changes — a crash, a safety car, an unexpected overtake?

We decided on ten different bets.

We had fun with it.

We did it for years.

Over time, I started believing that there must be more people like us — people who follow the entire F1 grid and have opinions about much more than who wins the race.

That made me want to understand something deeper:

> **What actually connects people to Formula 1?**

Is it the winner?

The rivalry?

The risk?

The uncertainty of not knowing what will happen next?

I imagined a platform where every fan could become part of that experience — a place to predict, compete, react and follow the race together.

Then something happened that changed the meaning of the idea completely.

My father passed away due to leukemia.

After losing him, I decided to take action.

I started building what had previously only existed in my mind — a world I could see clearly, but had never yet opened to anyone else.

That idea became **FR1BET**.

The final emotional line should be given visual importance:

> **Built to bring the grid to life — not just as a race to watch, but as a world to enter.**

This sentence should feel like the emotional conclusion of the story.

Do not dilute it.

Do not turn the story into generic startup language.

Do not make the story overly dramatic or sentimental.

Keep it honest, restrained and powerful.

# 17. FR1BET BRAND GUIDE — VISUAL SYSTEM TO CARRY INTO THIS SITE

The attached **FR1BET Brand Guide v1.0** is the visual reference for the website.

The personal site is still a **Wesley founder website**, not an FR1BET product page, but its visual language should feel like it belongs to the same world as FR1BET.

The brand guide describes the visual language as **telemetric, confident and high-contrast**, drawing from race-control displays, paddock signage and semaphore lights, with italic uppercase headlines and a single unmistakable red. fileciteturn3file0L16-L33

## Typography

Use **Inter as the single typeface across the entire website**.

The brand guide specifies these weights:

- Inter 400 — Regular
- Inter 500 — Medium
- Inter 600 — Semibold
- Inter 700 — Bold
- Inter 900 — Black
- Inter 900 Italic — Black Italic

The brand guide specifically uses **Inter Black Italic, uppercase, with tight tracking** for hero headlines. fileciteturn3file0L107-L128

Apply the hierarchy approximately as follows:

### Hero / Display
- Inter Black Italic
- 900 weight
- UPPERCASE
- Very tight tracking
- Tight line height
- Large editorial scale

### Section headlines
- Inter Black Italic or Inter Bold
- UPPERCASE where appropriate
- Strong, compact presentation

### Subheadings
- Inter Bold / Semibold
- Clear hierarchy

### Body copy
- Inter Regular
- Comfortable line height
- High readability
- Do not use italic styling for long paragraphs

### Labels / metadata
- Inter Bold or Semibold
- UPPERCASE
- Increased letter spacing
- Small size
- Use for things such as:
  - STORY
  - ORIGIN
  - FOUNDER
  - 01 / THE IDEA
  - 02 / THE TURNING POINT
  - NOW
  - NEXT

Do not introduce another display typeface. **One typeface, every weight.**
